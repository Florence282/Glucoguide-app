Diet Guide

General recommendations to help manage your blood sugar levels.

Recommended Foods

  • Non-starchy Vegetables: Spinach, broccoli, carrots
  • Whole Grains: Oats, quinoa, brown rice
  • Lean Proteins: Fish, beans, lentils, chicken
  • Healthy Fats: Nuts, avocados, olive oil
  • Fruits: Berries, apples (in moderation)

Foods to Limit

  • Sugary Drinks: Soda, sweet tea, fruit juice
  • Refined Carbs: White bread, white pasta, pastries
  • Processed Snacks: Chips, packaged crackers
  • Added Sugars: Candy, excessive honey
  • Trans Fats: Processed meats, fried fast food

Symptoms Guide

Learn to recognize physical signs of high and low blood sugar.

High Sugar (Hyperglycemia)

  • Excessive thirst or dry mouth
  • Frequent urination, especially at night
  • Fatigue and weakness
  • Blurred vision
  • Slow healing cuts or sores

Low Sugar (Hypoglycemia)

  • Shakiness, trembling, or jittery feeling
  • Sweating or chills
  • Dizziness or lightheadedness
  • Confusion or difficulty concentrating
  • Intense hunger or nausea

Active Movement

Simple, effective physical exercises to help manage your blood sugar levels gently.

Quick Tip: Always stretch before exercising and pause if you feel dizzy or short of breath. Keep a fast-acting carb (like juice) nearby just in case your sugar drops.

Brisk Walking

A simple, highly effective way to lower blood glucose and improve heart health without over-straining.

  • Frequency: 3-5 times a week
  • Duration: 30 minutes
  • Pace: Fast enough to increase heart rate, but you should still be able to hold a conversation.

Swimming / Water Aerobics

Water provides gentle resistance, giving you a full-body workout that is extremely easy on your joints.

  • Frequency: 2-3 times a week
  • Duration: 20-30 minutes
  • Focus: Cardiovascular endurance and muscle toning.

Light Cycling

Excellent for leg strength and cardiovascular health. Can be done outdoors or on a stationary bike.

  • Frequency: 3 times a week
  • Duration: 15-30 minutes
  • Tip: Ensure the bike seat height is comfortable to avoid knee strain.

Yoga & Stretching

Helps lower stress (which can spike blood sugar), improves flexibility, and builds core strength.

  • Frequency: Daily or every other day
  • Duration: 15-20 minutes
  • Focus: Deep breathing, gentle poses, and relaxation.

Compromising Conditions

Learn how other illnesses, medications, and stressors can unexpectedly spike your blood sugar levels.

Important: If your blood sugar is consistently high during an illness, or you are experiencing vomiting/diarrhea, contact your healthcare provider immediately. This is not medical advice.

Infections & Illness

When you are sick (like a cold, flu, or infection), your body releases hormones to fight the illness. These hormones raise blood sugar levels and increase insulin resistance.

  • Action: Check blood sugar more frequently (every 2-4 hours).
  • Action: Stay heavily hydrated to flush excess sugar.
  • Action: Continue taking diabetes medication unless advised otherwise by a doctor.

Physical & Mental Stress

Severe physical trauma, surgery, or extreme mental anxiety triggers the "fight or flight" response, dumping massive amounts of glucose and cortisol into the bloodstream.

  • Action: Practice the deep-breathing exercises found in the Exercise tab.
  • Action: Identify stress triggers and remove them if possible.
  • Action: Do not skip meals; keep your body fueled predictably.

Steroid Medications

Corticosteroids (often prescribed for asthma, arthritis, or severe allergies) are notorious for causing drastic spikes in blood glucose levels.

  • Action: Inform your doctor that you have diabetes before starting steroids.
  • Action: Monitor readings closely; your insulin/medication dose may need adjusting.

Lack of Sleep / Dehydration

Poor sleep decreases insulin sensitivity. Meanwhile, severe dehydration concentrates the sugar in your bloodstream, causing readings to spike artificially high.

  • Action: Aim for 7-8 hours of quality sleep.
  • Action: Use the daily Water Tracker on the Home dashboard to ensure you drink at least 8 glasses of water.

About GlucoGuide

GlucoGuide is a modern, privacy-first, fully client-side diabetes monitoring assistant. It is designed to replace traditional paper logs with a beautiful, accessible digital experience that requires no internet connection or external servers to function.

Key Features

  • Smart Monitoring: Instantly check your glucose levels against medical guidelines tailored for both Fasting and Post-Meal contexts. Receive dynamic lifestyle suggestions based on your results.
  • Monthly Calendar Tracking: Say goodbye to paper journals. Visually explore your entire history of readings plotted elegantly on an interactive calendar, complete with color-coded dot indicators denoting the severity of your logs on any given day.
  • Offline Data Persistence: All your health records, including hydration metrics, daily checklist states, and blood sugar histories, are stored entirely locally on your device's browser memory ensuring 100% data privacy.
  • Deep Accessibility: Crafted for universal access with large-text integration, high contrast color-blind modes, dark mode, and an elderly-oriented "Simple Mode" that strips away confusing dashboard widgets.
  • Smart Dashboards: Quickly track your daily water intake, calculate your Body Mass Index (BMI) dynamically, and enforce healthy habits with persistent daily checklists.
Medical Disclaimer: This application is a health-tracking simulation designed for educational awareness. It is NOT a diagnostic tool and does NOT prescribe medication. Always consult a certified medical professional or endocrinologist before making adjustments to your diabetes management routine.
